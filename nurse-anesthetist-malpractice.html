A medical perspective

Reviewing care with an understanding of the medicine.

Dr. Zaheer Shah’s experience as a physician informs his work as a malpractice attorney. He is familiar with the medical charts, imaging, and treatment records that help explain a patient’s course of care.

A claim involving a certified registered nurse anesthetist, or CRNA, calls for a review of that provider’s care and its connection to the injury. We listen to your account and examine the medical evidence.

Reviewing the care

Following the sequence of events.

01

Before the procedure

The available medical history, assessment, and planned care help provide context for the decisions that followed.

02

During anesthesia

The anesthesia record can help establish what medications were given, how the patient was monitored, and how changes were addressed.

03

After the procedure

Recovery records and subsequent treatment help explain when an injury became apparent and its consequences.

Our anesthesia error page discusses concerns involving medication, monitoring, and other aspects of anesthesia care.

Evaluating malpractice

Connecting a failure in care to an injury.

A serious complication does not by itself establish that a CRNA committed malpractice. A claim must address the standard of care applicable to the provider and whether a departure from that standard caused harm.

We use the treatment history and medical evidence to help assess those questions. The analysis is specific to the patient, the circumstances, and the care received.

Arizona law: A.R.S. § 12-563.
