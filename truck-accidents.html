Investigating the crash

Looking beyond the scene of the collision.

Truck accident cases can involve a driver, a trucking business, insurers, and several sources of evidence. A thorough investigation helps identify the people and organizations whose conduct should be examined.

01

The driver and the trip

Witness accounts, available driving records, and records of driving hours may help explain what happened before the crash.

02

The vehicle and its condition

Inspection and maintenance information can help establish whether the truck’s condition played a role.

03

The medical and crash evidence

Medical records and, when appropriate, accident reconstruction experts help connect the collision with the injuries and losses that followed.

Federal safety background: FMCSA information on carrier safety investigations.

Who may be responsible

Responsibility requires more than a company name.

The driver and a trucking company may both need to be considered when evaluating a claim. The driver’s relationship with the company, the work being performed, and each party’s conduct can affect the analysis.

Calling a driver an owner-operator or independent contractor does not, by itself, settle every question of responsibility. We examine the relationships and facts before deciding which parties a claim should address.

After a truck accident

Put your health first. Preserve what you can.

  1. Prioritize medical care

    Seek medical attention and follow the advice of your treating providers. Your care should come before gathering information.

  2. Document the scene safely

    If it is safe, photograph the vehicles, road conditions, debris, and visible injuries. Do not put yourself in danger to collect evidence.

  3. Keep contact information

    Save driver and insurance details, witness contact information, and the police report number if available.

  4. Keep your records together

    Retain medical records, bills, insurance correspondence, and notes about how the injuries affect your day-to-day life.

Injuries & recovery

Making the medical consequences clear.

The injuries described in truck accident cases can include broken bones, crushed limbs, brain injuries, internal injuries, cuts, and burns. Each person’s treatment and recovery are different.

Time away from work, pain, and changes to your independence are part of understanding the full impact of the crash.

Filing deadlines

Give your claim the time it needs.

Arizona personal injury claims generally have a two-year limitation period after the claim accrues. Exceptions and shorter deadlines may apply, including for claims involving public entities. An early review can help identify the applicable deadlines and the evidence needed for your case.

Arizona law: A.R.S. § 12-542; § 12-821.01; § 12-821.
