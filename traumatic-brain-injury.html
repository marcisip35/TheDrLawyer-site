Understanding brain injury

An injury to the way the brain works.

A traumatic brain injury, or TBI, affects how the brain functions. A blow or jolt to the head, or an impact that moves the head and brain rapidly, can cause a concussion or other brain injury.

Even an injury described as “mild” can have serious effects. Medical assessment is important when a brain injury is suspected; the diagnosis and recovery plan should come from a healthcare professional.

Medical background: CDC: About Mild TBI and Concussion.

The effect on everyday life

The changes are personal to you.

A brain injury can affect thinking, emotions, sleep, and physical wellbeing. The effects vary from person to person, which is why a claim should reflect your own experience.

Thinking and daily tasks

Changes in memory or concentration may affect the way you work, learn, or manage everyday responsibilities.

Health and routines

Symptoms may change sleep, comfort, and the activities you take part in. We listen to how those changes affect you and your family.

The medical records and your providers’ assessments help describe your needs. Information about work and daily activities can also show the impact that a diagnosis alone does not explain.

Exploring your legal options

Making room for the recovery ahead.

When a brain injury results from another person’s negligent, reckless, or intentional conduct, a legal claim may be available. The facts, the medical evidence, and the losses involved determine how a claim is evaluated.

Whether your injury has been described as mild, moderate, or severe, Dr. Shah and his team can discuss your circumstances and help you understand possible next steps.
